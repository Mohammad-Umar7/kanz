## identity
<!-- Image models like to "upgrade" the object into a new, perfect product. The reveal only works if the owner recognizes their own item, so identity comes first. -->
<!-- v2: v1 asked for "the exact shape" unconditionally, which contradicts projects that cut or reshape the item (a t-shirt into a tote bag, jeans into coasters). Shape is now kept only where the project leaves it, and new pieces must visibly come from this item. -->
This is the same physical object, transformed by hand, not a replacement or a nicer new one. Change only what the project changes and keep everything else: its color and material (glass keeps its tint and reflections, plastic its sheen, metal its finish, fabric its weave, print and wear, wood its grain), its shape, proportions and size in the frame wherever the project does not cut, fold or reshape it, and distinctive details such as label remnants, scratches, dents or chips unless the project removes, covers or paints over them. If the project cuts the item into new pieces, every piece is visibly made from this item: the same color, texture, print and wear.

## scene
<!-- The app lays this image over the original photo in a before/after slider, so both must line up. -->
Keep the same surface, background, lighting direction, color temperature and shadows as the photo, and the same camera angle, distance and framing, so the before and after pictures line up exactly. Added supplies (paint, twine, wire, soil, fabric, a candle) look like real, inexpensive materials from home, applied neatly by hand but not factory-perfect. Leave any other objects in the photo exactly where and as they are.
